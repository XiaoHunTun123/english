# Daily Vocabulary — 2026-09-30

以下 25 个词汇偏生僻但具有实用性，整体定位在英语内容覆盖率约 75%—90% 的范围。每个词包含音标、英文释义、中文释义、英文例句和中文翻译。

1. **adjudicate** /əˈdʒuːdɪkeɪt/  
   **English definition:** to make an official decision about a problem or dispute  
   **中文释义：** 裁决；判决  
   **Example:** An independent panel will adjudicate the dispute between the contractor and the city.  
   **中文：** 一个独立小组将裁决承包商与市政府之间的争端。

2. **aestheticism** /iːsˈθetɪsɪzəm/  
   **English definition:** an emphasis on beauty and artistic values, sometimes above practical concerns  
   **中文释义：** 唯美主义；审美至上  
   **Example:** The building reflects an aestheticism that values elegant forms over practical simplicity.  
   **中文：** 这座建筑体现了重视优美形式而非实用简洁的唯美主义。

3. **amalgamation** /əˌmælɡəˈmeɪʃən/  
   **English definition:** the process of combining two or more things into one  
   **中文释义：** 合并；融合  
   **Example:** The new department is an amalgamation of the research and engineering teams.  
   **中文：** 新部门由研究团队和工程团队合并而成。

4. **autodidact** /ˈɔːtoʊdaɪdækt/  
   **English definition:** a person who has learned a subject without being taught formally  
   **中文释义：** 自学者  
   **Example:** As an autodidact, she learned programming through books and open-source projects.  
   **中文：** 作为一名自学者，她通过书籍和开源项目学会了编程。

5. **burnish** /ˈbɜːrnɪʃ/  
   **English definition:** to improve or polish something, especially a reputation or image  
   **中文释义：** 磨光；提升；美化形象  
   **Example:** The charity campaign helped burnish the company’s public image.  
   **中文：** 这场慈善活动帮助提升了公司的公众形象。

6. **cabal** /kəˈbɑːl/  
   **English definition:** a small secret group working together, usually for a political or harmful purpose  
   **中文释义：** 秘密集团；阴谋小团体  
   **Example:** A cabal of senior officials was accused of manipulating the appointment process.  
   **中文：** 一个高级官员组成的秘密集团被指控操纵任命流程。

7. **calumny** /ˈkæləmni/  
   **English definition:** a false statement intended to damage someone’s reputation  
   **中文释义：** 诽谤；恶意中伤  
   **Example:** The journalist defended herself against the calumny spread on social media.  
   **中文：** 这名记者为自己辩护，反驳社交媒体上的恶意诽谤。

8. **carping** /ˈkɑːrpɪŋ/  
   **English definition:** continually criticizing small or unimportant faults  
   **中文释义：** 持续挑剔的；吹毛求疵的  
   **Example:** The editor’s carping comments distracted the team from the main argument.  
   **中文：** 编辑不断挑剔的评论使团队偏离了主要论点。

9. **castigate** /ˈkæstɪɡeɪt/  
   **English definition:** to criticize or punish someone severely  
   **中文释义：** 严厉批评；惩罚  
   **Example:** The regulator castigated the company for concealing safety problems.  
   **中文：** 监管机构严厉批评该公司隐瞒安全问题。

10. **circumlocution** /ˌsɜːrkəmləˈkjuːʃən/  
    **English definition:** an indirect way of speaking that uses more words than necessary  
    **中文释义：** 迂回说法；绕圈子  
    **Example:** The manager’s circumlocution made it difficult to understand the actual decision.  
    **中文：** 经理绕圈子的说法让人难以理解实际决定。

11. **collusion** /kəˈluːʒən/  
    **English definition:** secret cooperation for an illegal or dishonest purpose  
    **中文释义：** 共谋；勾结  
    **Example:** The investigation found evidence of collusion between the suppliers.  
    **中文：** 调查发现供应商之间存在勾结的证据。

12. **commiserate** /kəˈmɪzəreɪt/  
    **English definition:** to express sympathy with someone who is experiencing difficulty  
    **中文释义：** 同情；慰问  
    **Example:** Colleagues gathered to commiserate with her after the project was canceled.  
    **中文：** 项目取消后，同事们聚在一起安慰她。

13. **concordat** /kənˈkɔːrdæt/  
    **English definition:** a formal agreement, especially between groups with different interests  
    **中文释义：** 正式协定；协议  
    **Example:** The two organizations signed a concordat on sharing research data.  
    **中文：** 两个组织签署了共享研究数据的正式协议。

14. **consummate** /ˈkɑːnsəmət/  
    **English definition:** showing very high skill or the greatest possible degree of a quality  
    **中文释义：** 技艺精湛的；完美的  
    **Example:** She is a consummate professional who remains calm under pressure.  
    **中文：** 她是一位技艺精湛的专业人士，在压力下仍能保持冷静。

15. **coterie** /ˈkoʊtəri/  
    **English definition:** a small close-knit group with shared interests or activities  
    **中文释义：** 小圈子；志趣相投的小团体  
    **Example:** A coterie of independent designers organized the exhibition.  
    **中文：** 一群独立设计师组成的小圈子组织了这次展览。

16. **countermand** /ˌkaʊntərˈmænd/  
    **English definition:** to cancel or reverse an order or instruction  
    **中文释义：** 撤销命令；取消指示  
    **Example:** The commander countermanded the order after receiving new intelligence.  
    **中文：** 指挥官收到新的情报后撤销了命令。

17. **credulity** /krəˈduːləti/  
    **English definition:** a tendency to believe things too easily  
    **中文释义：** 轻信；易受骗  
    **Example:** The scam depended on the credulity of people who wanted quick profits.  
    **中文：** 这场骗局利用了那些想快速获利之人的轻信。

18. **declaim** /dɪˈkleɪm/  
    **English definition:** to speak or read aloud in a dramatic and forceful way  
    **中文释义：** 慷慨陈词；朗诵  
    **Example:** The activist declaimed against the policy in front of the crowded hall.  
    **中文：** 这名活动家在拥挤的大厅前慷慨陈词，反对这项政策。

19. **decorous** /ˈdekərəs/  
    **English definition:** polite, dignified, and socially appropriate  
    **中文释义：** 端庄得体的；合乎礼仪的  
    **Example:** The discussion remained decorous despite strong disagreement.  
    **中文：** 尽管分歧很大，讨论仍保持了得体的氛围。

20. **defray** /dɪˈfreɪ/  
    **English definition:** to provide money to pay for a cost or expense  
    **中文释义：** 支付；承担费用  
    **Example:** A grant helped defray the cost of the students’ field research.  
    **中文：** 一笔助学金帮助支付了学生实地研究的费用。

21. **deign** /deɪn/  
    **English definition:** to do something that one considers beneath one’s dignity  
    **中文释义：** 屈尊；俯就做某事  
    **Example:** The executive did not deign to answer the employee’s reasonable question.  
    **中文：** 那位高管不屑回答员工合理的问题。

22. **dissolute** /ˈdɪsəluːt/  
    **English definition:** lacking moral discipline and living in a careless or self-indulgent way  
    **中文释义：** 放荡的；生活放纵的  
    **Example:** The novel follows a dissolute heir who gradually recognizes the harm he has caused.  
    **中文：** 这部小说讲述了一位生活放纵的继承人逐渐认识到自己造成的伤害。

23. **epigram** /ˈepɪɡræm/  
    **English definition:** a short, clever statement or poem expressing an idea memorably  
    **中文释义：** 警句；隽语  
    **Example:** The speaker ended the presentation with an epigram about curiosity.  
    **中文：** 演讲者用一句关于好奇心的警句结束了演讲。

24. **epistemic** /ˌepɪˈstiːmɪk/  
    **English definition:** relating to knowledge, evidence, or the degree of certainty about something  
    **中文释义：** 认识论的；与知识和确定性有关的  
    **Example:** The study distinguishes between moral disagreement and epistemic uncertainty.  
    **中文：** 这项研究区分了道德分歧与认识上的不确定性。

25. **excoriate** /ˈekskɔːrieɪt/  
    **English definition:** to criticize someone or something very severely  
    **中文释义：** 严厉抨击；痛斥  
    **Example:** Editorials excoriated the company for ignoring repeated safety warnings.  
    **中文：** 多家社论严厉抨击该公司无视反复出现的安全警告。
