# Daily Vocabulary — 2026-09-05

今日词汇定位：偏生僻但实用，整体适合英语内容覆盖率约 75%—90% 的学习阶段。每个词提供音标、英释、中释及双语例句。

1. **accede** /əkˈsiːd/ — agree to a request or accept a position；同意请求或接受职位；答应；就任
   - **Example:** The board finally acceded to the workers’ demands.
   - **中文：** 董事会最终答应了工人的要求。

2. **acrid** /ˈækrɪd/ — having a strong, unpleasant smell or taste；气味或味道强烈刺鼻的；辛辣刺鼻的
   - **Example:** An acrid smell filled the room after the wires overheated.
   - **中文：** 电线过热后，房间里充满了刺鼻的气味。

3. **aberrant** /æˈberənt/ — departing from what is normal or expected；偏离正常或预期的；异常的
   - **Example:** The scientist repeated the test to check whether the result was aberrant.
   - **中文：** 科学家重复了测试，以确认这个结果是否异常。

4. **affront** /əˈfrʌnt/ — an action or remark that causes offense；冒犯性的行为或言论；侮辱；冒犯
   - **Example:** She regarded the public accusation as a serious affront to her reputation.
   - **中文：** 她认为公开指责严重损害了自己的声誉。

5. **animosity** /ˌænɪˈmɑːsəti/ — strong hostility or dislike；强烈的敌意或厌恶；敌意
   - **Example:** Years of animosity made cooperation between the two groups difficult.
   - **中文：** 多年的敌意使两个团体之间难以合作。

6. **apprise** /əˈpraɪz/ — inform someone about something；把某事告知某人；通知
   - **Example:** Please apprise me of any changes to the schedule.
   - **中文：** 日程有任何变化，请及时通知我。

7. **audacious** /ɔːˈdeɪʃəs/ — willing to take bold or risky action；敢于采取大胆或冒险行动的；大胆的
   - **Example:** The startup proposed an audacious plan to redesign the entire industry.
   - **中文：** 这家初创公司提出了一个大胆的计划，试图重塑整个行业。

8. **coalesce** /ˌkoʊəˈles/ — come together to form one group or whole；聚集并形成一个整体；联合
   - **Example:** Several small organizations coalesced around a common goal.
   - **中文：** 几个小型组织围绕共同目标联合起来。

9. **complicit** /kəmˈplɪsɪt/ — involved in or responsible for wrongdoing；参与或应对错误行为负责的；共谋的
   - **Example:** Anyone who conceals the evidence is complicit in the deception.
   - **中文：** 任何隐瞒证据的人都参与了这场欺骗。

10. **conciliatory** /kənˈsɪliətɔːri/ — intended to make peace or reduce conflict；旨在和解或减少冲突的；安抚的
    - **Example:** Her conciliatory tone helped both sides return to the negotiation table.
    - **中文：** 她安抚性的语气帮助双方重新回到了谈判桌前。

11. **daunt** /dɔːnt/ — make someone feel intimidated or less confident；使某人感到畏缩或失去信心；使胆怯
    - **Example:** The scale of the project did not daunt the experienced engineer.
    - **中文：** 项目的规模没有让那位经验丰富的工程师退缩。

12. **deride** /dɪˈraɪd/ — mock or express contempt for someone or something；嘲笑或蔑视某人或某事；嘲弄
    - **Example:** Critics derided the proposal before examining its evidence.
    - **中文：** 批评者还没审视证据，就嘲弄了这项提议。

13. **dissemble** /dɪˈsembəl/ — conceal one’s true feelings or intentions；隐藏真实感受或意图；掩饰
    - **Example:** He tried to dissemble, but his hesitation was obvious.
    - **中文：** 他试图掩饰，但他的犹豫显而易见。

14. **dogmatic** /dɔːɡˈmætɪk/ — expressing opinions as unquestionably true；把观点说成绝对正确的；教条的
    - **Example:** A dogmatic approach can prevent a team from considering useful alternatives.
    - **中文：** 教条式的方法可能阻碍团队考虑有用的替代方案。

15. **ephemeral** /ɪˈfemərəl/ — lasting for a very short time；持续时间很短的；短暂的
    - **Example:** Online popularity can be surprisingly ephemeral.
    - **中文：** 网络上的受欢迎程度可能短暂得令人惊讶。

16. **furtive** /ˈfɜːrtɪv/ — secretive and intended to avoid notice；秘密进行且试图避免注意的；偷偷摸摸的
    - **Example:** He gave the room a furtive glance before opening the package.
    - **中文：** 他偷偷扫视了一眼房间，才打开包裹。

17. **hackneyed** /ˈhæknid/ — overused and therefore lacking freshness；因使用过度而缺乏新意的；陈腐的
    - **Example:** The speech relied on hackneyed phrases instead of concrete ideas.
    - **中文：** 这篇演讲依赖陈腐的套话，而不是具体的想法。

18. **inadvertent** /ˌɪnədˈvɜːrtənt/ — unintentional or accidental；非故意的或偶然的；无意的
    - **Example:** The email contained an inadvertent error in the financial figures.
    - **中文：** 这封邮件中的财务数字有一个无意造成的错误。

19. **inexorable** /ɪnˈeksərəbəl/ — impossible to stop or prevent；无法停止或阻止的；不可阻挡的
    - **Example:** The inexorable rise of costs forced the company to revise its plans.
    - **中文：** 成本不断上升，迫使公司修改了计划。

20. **laconic** /ləˈkɑːnɪk/ — using very few words；使用极少词语的；简洁的
    - **Example:** His laconic reply made it difficult to tell what he really thought.
    - **中文：** 他简短的回答让人难以判断他的真实想法。

21. **proclivity** /proʊˈklɪvəti/ — a natural tendency or preference；自然的倾向或偏好；癖好
    - **Example:** She has a proclivity for solving complicated problems.
    - **中文：** 她天生喜欢解决复杂的问题。

22. **succinct** /səkˈsɪŋkt/ — expressed clearly in few words；用很少的话清楚表达的；简洁的
    - **Example:** Please give a succinct summary of the findings.
    - **中文：** 请简明扼要地总结研究结果。

23. **tenable** /ˈtenəbəl/ — reasonable and able to be defended；合理且能够被辩护的；站得住脚的
    - **Example:** The evidence does not make that conclusion tenable.
    - **中文：** 这些证据不足以让那个结论站得住脚。

24. **vacillate** /ˈvæsəleɪt/ — repeatedly change between different choices or opinions；在不同选择或观点之间反复改变；摇摆不定
    - **Example:** Investors may vacillate when the market outlook is unclear.
    - **中文：** 市场前景不明朗时，投资者可能会摇摆不定。

25. **abstruse** /əbˈstruːs/ — difficult to understand because it is highly specialized or complex；因高度专业或复杂而难以理解的；深奥的
    - **Example:** The lecture introduced an abstruse theory with several practical applications.
    - **中文：** 这场讲座介绍了一种深奥但有多种实际应用的理论。
